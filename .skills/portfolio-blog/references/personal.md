# Mode: Personal essay

Modelled on `80-20-rule.md` and `forher.md`. Used sparingly — two posts out of 41.

**Target: 600–800 words. First person. No code. Warm, not clinical.**

Two sub-flavours, and they are different enough to be worth naming:

- **Reflective / book-derived** (`80-20-rule`) — structured, sectioned, actionable. Teaches a principle and ends with a numbered key-points list.
- **Lyrical / confessional** (`forher`) — unbroken prose, second person addressing an absent person, imagery, no headings at all.

Ask which one if the topic allows either. "Lessons I learned from X" is reflective; anything about a person or a feeling is lyrical.

## Reflective flavour

`##` headings numbered as `## 1) 80/20: The Basics`, `## 2) You Can Be great`. Note the lowercase `g` — the corpus has typos like this and they should not be reproduced; write `Great`.

Each section: a claim, then the reasoning, then bullets that unpack it. Sub-bullets are common and are indented two spaces.

Ends with a `## KEY POINTS:` list that names each section in one line. That recap is a feature, not padding — it is how the reader who skimmed gets the value.

Typographic habits in this flavour: `*italics*` for emphasis, `**bold**` for the load-bearing claim, arrows (`→`, `-`) for cause-effect. Fragments and rhetorical questions are fine.

## Lyrical flavour

One wall of prose. `##` never appears. Paragraphs are long (80–120 words), sentences are long and comma-spliced, and the register is elevated — "the weight of unmet expectations, unresolved grief, and constant disappointment presses down on your soul."

Rules for doing this without it reading as parody:
- Concrete sensory detail carries the abstraction. `The toughest is nighttime. The silence sharpens everything.`
- Repetition is a tool: `If only, you consider. If only you had chosen another course.`
- Second person for the reader-as-subject, first person for the writer's own confession. `forher` mixes both and it works.
- End on a turn toward hope, but a qualified one. `The darkest darkness lift too, finally.` Do not resolve cleanly.

This flavour is the hardest to fake and the easiest to overdo. If the user has not given you real material — a specific memory, a specific person, a specific loss — write the reflective flavour instead and say why.

## Anti-patterns

- ❌ Manufactured trauma, or essaying feelings the user never mentioned
- ❌ Bullet points in the lyrical flavour
- ❌ A neat moral at the end of either flavour
- ❌ Emoji (the sign-offs belong to other modes)
- ❌ Therapy-speak (`holding space`, `doing the work`, `unpacking`)
- ❌ Naming real people, employers, or institutions without being told to
